1.   EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

ARGUS contains two deliberative protocols for multi-agent reasoning. ARISTOTLE
orchestrates a sequential specialist team through structured rounds of evidence
and rebuttal, delivering a deeply analyzed verdict from a fixed topology.
AGORA convenes a dynamically composed Senate where N senators deliberate
concurrently under formal procedural rules — a parliament of agents. Both are
fundamentally inspired by the ideal of the deliberative assembly: a room where
reasoned argument converges on truth through dialogue.

But there is a different model of adversarial reasoning — one that is older,
starker, and in many ways more honest about the nature of contested truth.
That model is the battle. Not metaphorically. Structurally.

HANNIBAL — the Hierarchical Adversarial Network for Nested Intelligence Battles
and Logic — is the third ARGUS deliberative protocol. It does not convene a
Senate. It deploys armies.

When a proposition enters HANNIBAL, it is not assigned to a panel of debaters.
It is analyzed for its epistemic depth and polarity structure. Based on that
analysis, HANNIBAL generates two or more Forces — organized armies of agents —
and deploys them against each other on structured War Fronts. Each Force
fights to win: to make its position the dominant verdict through superior
evidence, stronger logic, and more decisive argument. The battle is not a
metaphor for debate. It IS the debate, re-architected as organized combat.

The core data structure that governs this combat is the Tournament Tree —
a hierarchical bracket that makes the path from individual agent clashes all
the way to the final campaign verdict both computationally tractable and
visually legible. Every clash, at every level, produces a winner whose
position propagates upward through the bracket until a single dominant
position emerges at the root: the Campaign Verdict.

The Proposition Depth Analyzer determines how many fronts are opened.
Simple propositions → Bipolar: two forces, one front. Complex, multi-
dimensional propositions → Multipolar: three or more forces, multiple
simultaneous fronts, each front its own tournament sub-tree. The multipolar
case is governed by HANNIBAL's CANNAE Engine — named for Hannibal Barca's
greatest battle, where encirclement of the enemy from multiple angles
produced a decisive result that simple frontal assault could never achieve.

Alongside the existing C-DAG visualization that ARGUS already provides for
argument structure, HANNIBAL introduces a second simultaneous visualization:
the Tournament Tree display — a live, updating bracket that shows every
skirmish, engagement, and theatre result as the battle unfolds. The user
sees both: the epistemic structure (C-DAG) and the battle structure
(Tournament Tree), rendered in the same interface, updating in real time.

HANNIBAL is launched with a single proposition:

  argus hannibal run

The Forces are generated. The fronts are drawn. The battle begins.

Caesar, and the Duke of Wellington each independently named him the supreme
military genius of antiquity. But Hannibal's greatness was not in his
individual victories — it was in the architectural novelty of how he
engineered conflict itself.

3.   THE INTELLECTUAL LINEAGE — ARISTOTLE, AGORA, AND THE BATTLEFIELD GAP
================================================================================

3.1  ARISTOTLE — What It Does and Where It Stops
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

ARISTOTLE is a masterwork of orchestrated sequential debate. Its five-layer
architecture — Framing, Topology, Execution, Visualization, Synthesis —
transforms any natural-language question into a fully autonomous, visualized,
auditable debate with a clear verdict. Its Full Lifecycle DAG is the definitive
visualization of multi-agent argument structure.

ARISTOTLE's structural constraint: it is a fixed team engaging a proposition
as a shared project. Agents do not fight each other — they collectively
investigate the proposition from different angles. The Moderator orchestrates.
The Specialists gather. The Refuters challenge. The Jury decides. This is
collaborative analytical work dressed in adversarial language. The "refuter"
does not want to destroy the proposition — they test it. The jury does not
choose a winner — they aggregate evidence. It is, at its core, a structured
research process, not a battle.

For many propositions, this is exactly right. A proposition about drug
efficacy or historical causation benefits from a collaborative investigative
team. But some propositions are genuinely zero-sum: either this policy works
or it does not. Either this cause is real or it is not. Either this claim
can be defended or it collapses under adversarial pressure. For these
propositions, the collaborative research model is the wrong architecture.
What is needed is a genuine fight — where the correct position wins because
it was stronger, not because the jury aggregated everyone's weighted opinion.

3.2  AGORA — What It Does and Where It Stops
──────────────────────────────────────────────

AGORA is the procedural parliament of multi-agent AI. Its Balance Mandate,
Evidence Docket, Socratic Engine, and Minority Report Engine create the most
formally governed deliberative assembly in the existing framework landscape.
Its Senate Generation Engine ensures that no proposition ever faces the same
Senate twice, and that balance is architectural.

AGORA's structural constraint: it is still a deliberative assembly. Senators
argue, challenge, and submit evidence — but they all operate within a single
chamber under shared procedural rules. There are no armies. There are no
fronts. There is no battlefield. Coalition formation happens organically and
is detected and surfaced — but it is not the primary organizing principle.
The Senate's goal is convergence on a verdict through deliberation. Even the
minority report is produced after the fact, as documentation, not as a force
that fought to win.

AGORA produces a governed, accountable deliberation. HANNIBAL produces a
governed, accountable battle. These are genuinely different things.

3.3  The Existing Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) Landscape
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Survey of the 2025-2026 MAD literature reveals the following consistent
pattern: existing adversarial frameworks (Div-MAD, A-HMAD, iMAD, DMAD)
all use one of two paradigms —

  PARADIGM 1 — COLLABORATIVE: Agents share information and converge.
  PARADIGM 2 — ADVERSARIAL: Agents are assigned opposing roles and argue.

Neither paradigm has:
  — An organized military Force structure for agent deployment
  — A Tournament Tree as the primary organizing data structure
  — A Proposition Depth Analyzer that scales fronts from bipolar to multipolar
  — A CANNAE-style multipolar encirclement mechanism
  — A hierarchical battle resolution system (Skirmish → Engagement →
    Theatre → Campaign) with winner propagation at each level
  — Military tactical roles (Vanguard, Flanking, Intelligence, Reserve)
  — A dedicated Tournament Tree visualization alongside the C-DAG
  — Force-level posterior tracking alongside node-level belief propagation
  — A War Room interface showing battlefield state in real time

The gap is consistent and documented. The battlefield protocol does not exist.

3.4  Confirmed Research Gap
─────────────────────────────

Wu et al. (Nov 2025) established that:
  — "Productive initial chaos" (genuine initial disagreement) improves
    post-debate outcomes
  — Argument validity weighting outperforms simple majority
  — Diversity without extreme positions produces the best results

HANNIBAL operationalizes all three findings:
  — Forces are initialized with maximally opposing priors (productive chaos)
  — Battle resolution uses evidence quality (EVID-Q) not agent count
  — Force composition uses a heterogeneous deployment algorithm ensuring
    diverse but competent agents on each side

No existing framework does all three simultaneously within a tournament
bracket architecture. HANNIBAL is the first.

4.   THE FUNDAMENTAL PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

The existing ARGUS deliberative protocols — ARISTOTLE and AGORA — share a
foundational organizational metaphor: the assembly. Agents gather in a room
(real or virtual) and reason together toward a verdict. This is the right
model for propositions that are investigative in nature — where truth emerges
from collaborative evidence accumulation and qualified synthesis.

But several classes of propositions are not investigative in nature.
They are genuinely zero-sum competitive claims:

  "Policy X is better than Policy Y at achieving outcome Z"
  "The evidence for Hypothesis A is stronger than the evidence for Hypothesis B"
  "Interpretation P of this event is more defensible than Interpretation Q"
  "The risk of action R outweighs its benefits"

For these propositions, the assembly metaphor produces a structural problem:
collaborative deliberation is biased toward compromise and synthesis. If the
correct answer is that one side is decisively right and the other is decisively
wrong, a Senate or a specialist team will tend to produce a hedged, qualified,
"both sides have merit" verdict — because their architecture rewards convergence.

HANNIBAL exists to solve this problem:

  PROBLEM 1 — ASSEMBLY METAPHOR BIAS TOWARD COMPROMISE
  The deliberative assembly — Senate, specialist team, jury — structurally
  rewards convergence. For zero-sum propositions, convergence is wrong.
  HANNIBAL's battle architecture structurally rewards the dominance of the
  stronger epistemic position, not the synthesis of all positions.

  PROBLEM 2 — NO HIERARCHICAL BATTLE RESOLUTION STRUCTURE
  Existing adversarial MAD frameworks treat all argument clashes as
  equivalent. A claim challenged by a counter-claim is one "round."
  HANNIBAL introduces hierarchical resolution: Skirmishes resolve to
  Engagements, Engagements resolve to Theatre results, Theatres resolve
  to Campaign Verdicts. The resolution structure is explicit, transparent,
  and traceable at every level.

  PROBLEM 3 — BIPOLAR ONLY, NO MULTIPOLAR FRONT SCALING
  All existing adversarial frameworks are binary: FOR vs. AGAINST.
  Real propositions often have more than two legitimate positions —
  position A, position B, and a qualified position C that is neither.
  HANNIBAL's Proposition Depth Analyzer detects this and opens a third
  (or fourth) front. The multipolar battle is structurally different from
  a bipolar debate, and no existing framework handles it.

  PROBLEM 4 — NO TOURNAMENT TREE DATA STRUCTURE
  Existing frameworks have no hierarchical structure for aggregating
  individual argument clashes into a progressively resolved bracket.
  The tournament tree is the natural structure for this — borrowed from
  competitive tournaments where individual match results propagate through
  a bracket to a champion. No existing MAD framework uses it.

  PROBLEM 5 — NO BATTLEFIELD VISUALIZATION
  The C-DAG shows argument structure. The Timeline shows temporal
  progression. Neither shows what a battlefield looks like: which forces
  occupy which fronts, how the tournament bracket is resolving, where
  the fighting is heaviest. HANNIBAL's War Room visualization fills this gap.

  PROBLEM 6 — NO FORCE-LEVEL POSTERIOR TRACKING
  Bayesian belief propagation in ARGUS operates at the node level within the
  C-DAG. HANNIBAL introduces Force-level posterior tracking: at any moment,
  the overall epistemic strength of each Force — the army-level posterior —
  is computed from its constituent evidence victories and defeats. This is
  a higher-order belief metric that no existing framework exposes.

  PROBLEM 7 — NO TACTICAL ROLE ARCHITECTURE FOR AGENT DEPLOYMENT
  Existing frameworks assign agents to functional roles (specialist, refuter,
  jury). These roles describe what the agent does epistemically. HANNIBAL
  assigns agents to tactical roles (Vanguard, Flanking, Intelligence, Reserve,
  Field Marshal). These describe the agent's position in the battle structure
  and their relationship to the enemy force — an entirely different organizing
  principle that enables coordination patterns impossible in role-only systems.

HANNIBAL addresses all seven problems in an integrated architecture.

5.   THE CORE ARCHITECTURAL IDEA — SENATE vs. BATTLEFIELD
================================================================================

The central conceptual shift in HANNIBAL can be expressed in one sentence:
Instead of agents reasoning toward a shared verdict, HANNIBAL deploys agents
as opposing forces fighting for a verdict.

This shift has seven architectural consequences:

  CONSEQUENCE 1 — AGENTS BELONG TO FORCES, NOT TO A SHARED CHAMBER
  Every agent in a HANNIBAL session is assigned to exactly one Force.
  The Proposition Force (PF) is composed of agents whose mandate is to
  demonstrate the proposition is TRUE. The Opposition Force (OF) is composed
  of agents whose mandate is to demonstrate the proposition is FALSE.
  In multipolar sessions, additional Faction Forces (FF) hold distinct
  qualified positions. Agents do not observe from a neutral position —
  they fight from a committed one.

  CONSEQUENCE 2 — EVIDENCE IS MUNITION, NOT TESTIMONY
  In ARISTOTLE and AGORA, evidence is submitted by an agent who has
  researched the topic and now informs the group. In HANNIBAL, evidence
  is deployed by an agent whose Force needs it to win a specific Skirmish.
  The same evidence quality metrics (EVID-Q, source quality, confidence)
  apply — but the strategic context is different. Evidence selection is
  tactical, not just epistemic.

  CONSEQUENCE 3 — COMBAT, NOT DIALOGUE
  When a Vanguard agent of the Proposition Force submits a claim, a
  corresponding Flanking agent of the Opposition Force does not "challenge"
  it in the manner of AGORA's Evidence Challenge Protocol. They engage it
  in direct combat: Skirmish initiated, round of direct exchange, Skirmish
  winner determined by relative epistemic strength (EVID-Q × logical
  coherence × confidence). This is not dialogue. It is structured combat
  with a winner and a loser.

  CONSEQUENCE 4 — THE TOURNAMENT TREE IS THE CAMPAIGN MAP
  All combat is organized through the Tournament Tree. Before the battle
  begins, the Tree is pre-specified: how many Theatres, how many Engagements
  per Theatre, how many Skirmishes per Engagement, which agent types fight
  which, and in what order. The battle executes the tree. As Skirmishes
  resolve, their results propagate upward to Engagements. As Engagements
  resolve, their results propagate to Theatres. As Theatres resolve, the
  Campaign Verdict emerges at the root.

  CONSEQUENCE 5 — FORCE COMMANDERS DIRECT TACTICAL DECISIONS
  Each Force has a Commander agent whose role is not to submit evidence but
  to direct which Skirmishes to prioritize, which Intelligence reports to
  act on, when to deploy Reserve agents, and how to allocate the Force's
  evidence-gathering resources across the active Engagements. This is a
  meta-tactical layer above individual agent behavior that has no equivalent
  in any existing MAD framework.

  CONSEQUENCE 6 — THE PROPOSITION DEPTH DETERMINES THE BATTLE TOPOLOGY
  Simple propositions → one Theatre, bipolar (PF vs. OF), one tournament
  bracket resolving from multiple Engagements to one Campaign Verdict.
  Complex propositions → multiple Theatres, multipolar (PF vs. OF vs. FF-1
  vs. FF-2...), separate tournament sub-trees per Theatre, aggregated into
  the campaign via the CANNAE Engine. The battle topology is derived from
  the proposition, not pre-set.

  CONSEQUENCE 7 — THE WINNER OF THE CAMPAIGN IS THE VERDICT
  There is no Jury that aggregates all evidence and renders a balanced verdict.
  The Force that wins the Campaign — by winning more Theatres, by winning
  the decisive Engagements, by having the superior posterior at the root of
  the Tournament Tree — IS the verdict. Their position is adopted as the
  Campaign Conclusion. The losing Force's strongest surviving arguments are
  documented in the Campaign Minority Record (see Section 14), but the verdict
  belongs to the winner. This is structurally different from any existing system.

6.   THE PROPOSITION DEPTH ANALYZER — BIPOLAR TO MULTIPOLAR SCALING
================================================================================

The Proposition Depth Analyzer (PDA) is HANNIBAL's opening module. It
runs before any Force is deployed. Its job: determine how many fronts
this proposition deserves.

6.1  Input: The Proposition
─────────────────────────────

Any natural-language proposition submitted to HANNIBAL is processed through
five analytical steps:

  STEP 1 — POLARITY STRUCTURE DETECTION
  Does the proposition have a natural binary structure (TRUE / FALSE) or
  does it have multiple legitimate non-binary positions?

    Binary example:
    "The 2008 financial crisis was primarily caused by subprime mortgage risk"
    → Two defensible positions: YES or NO. Bipolar structure.

    Multi-position example:
    "Artificial intelligence will be net beneficial for employment"
    → Defensible positions: YES / NO / DEPENDS ON SECTOR / DEPENDS ON TIMEFRAME
    → Multiple legitimate non-collapsible positions. Multipolar structure.

  STEP 2 — EPISTEMIC DEPTH SCORING
  Rate the proposition on three axes, each 0.0 to 1.0:
    — FACTUAL DEPTH: How much primary empirical evidence exists?
    — NORMATIVE DEPTH: How much of the dispute is about values vs. facts?
    — INFERENTIAL DEPTH: How long is the logical chain from evidence to claim?

  The aggregate Depth Score determines Tournament Tree height:
    Depth < 0.35  → Tree Height 2 (Skirmish → Campaign) — Quick Battle
    Depth 0.35-0.65 → Tree Height 3 (Skirmish → Engagement → Campaign)
    Depth 0.65-0.85 → Tree Height 4 (Skirmish → Engagement → Theatre → Campaign)
    Depth > 0.85   → Tree Height 5 (Skirmish → Engagement → Theatre →
                                     Campaign Theatre → Grand Campaign)

  STEP 3 — FRONT COUNT DETERMINATION
  Combining Polarity Structure with Epistemic Depth:

    Bipolar, any depth: 2 Forces, 1 set of Fronts
    Tripolar (3 positions), low depth: 3 Forces, round-robin tournament
    Tripolar, high depth: 3 Forces, 3 bilateral Fronts (each pair fights)
    Quadrupolar+: CANNAE Engine activated (see Section 11)

  STEP 4 — THEATRE SPECIFICATION
  For multi-front battles, each distinct epistemic dimension of the
  proposition becomes a Theatre. A Theatre is a topically bounded battle
  space where specific sub-arguments of the proposition are contested.

    Example — Proposition: "Universal Basic Income would reduce poverty"
    Theatre 1 — Economic efficacy: Does UBI reduce poverty in economic models?
    Theatre 2 — Behavioral effects: Does UBI affect work incentives?
    Theatre 3 — Fiscal sustainability: Can governments afford UBI?
    Theatre 4 — Empirical precedent: What pilot programs show

  STEP 5 — FORCE SIZE CALIBRATION
  Each Force is sized based on the battle topology:
    Minimum Force size: 3 agents (1 Vanguard, 1 Flanking, 1 Commander)
    Typical Force size: 5-7 agents
    Maximum Force size (high depth, multipolar): 12 agents
    Total agent count is the sum of all Force sizes across all Fronts

6.2  Output: The Battle Map
────────────────────────────

The PDA outputs a Battle Map: a complete specification of the upcoming
campaign, including:
  — Number of Forces and their designations (PF, OF, FF-1...FF-n)
  — Number of Theatres and their topical scope
  — Tournament Tree structure (height, width, node count)
  — Force size per Theatre
  — Estimated Skirmish count and campaign duration
  — Front polarity mapping (which Forces fight on which Fronts)
  — CANNAE Engine activation flag (YES/NO for multipolar encirclement)

The Battle Map is shown to the user as a pre-battle briefing before any
agent is deployed. The user may approve, modify, or request reanalysis.

7.   FORCE ARCHITECTURE — ARMIES, FRONTS, AND MILITARY ROLES
================================================================================

7.1  Force Types
─────────────────

  PROPOSITION FORCE (PF)
  The army fighting to demonstrate the proposition is TRUE or defensible.
  All PF agents are mandated to find, evaluate, and deploy evidence that
  supports the proposition. They initiate Skirmishes from a position of
  advocacy — the strongest possible case FOR.

  OPPOSITION FORCE (OF)
  The army fighting to demonstrate the proposition is FALSE or indefensible.
  All OF agents are mandated to find, evaluate, and deploy evidence that
  challenges the proposition. Their goal is to destroy PF's evidence base
  and establish an alternative.

  FACTION FORCE (FF-n)  [Multipolar only]
  Each FF holds a qualified or alternative position that is neither full
  support nor full opposition. FF agents fight to establish their specific
  qualified verdict as the dominant outcome. In the multipolar tournament,
  FF forces fight both PF and OF — and each other — across the configured
  Front structure.

7.2  Military Role Taxonomy
─────────────────────────────

Every agent in every Force occupies exactly one of five tactical roles.
These roles are assigned by the Force Deployment Engine based on the
Battle Map and the agent's epistemic configuration.

  ROLE 1 — COMMANDER (C)
  ─────────────────────────
  One Commander per Force. The Commander does not submit evidence. They
  direct the Force's tactical decisions across the battle:
    — Which Skirmishes to prioritize
    — When to deploy Reserve agents
    — How to respond to Intelligence reports
    — Whether to request a Tactical Redirect (if a Theatre is being lost)
    — When to invoke the Armistice Protocol
  The Commander corresponds to ARISTOTLE's ORCHESTRATOR within a single
  Force. They have access to the full Battle Map and all Intelligence
  reports. Their directives are issued to their Force agents as structured
  tactical orders that constrain evidence-gathering focus.

  ROLE 2 — VANGUARD (V)
  ────────────────────────
  Two to four Vanguard agents per Force. These are the primary evidence
  submitters — the frontline fighters. Each Vanguard is assigned to a
  specific Theatre and is responsible for initiating Skirmishes in that
  Theatre with the strongest available evidence for their Force's position.
  Vanguard agents correspond functionally to ARISTOTLE's Specialists, but
  they are goal-directed toward victory, not toward balance.

  VANGUARD CONFIGURATION:
    — Domain expertise: per-Theatre specialization
    — Evidence source priority: calibrated to Theatre topic
    — Prior: set to maximum commitment to Force position (0.85-0.95 for PF;
      0.05-0.15 for OF) — ensuring "productive initial chaos" per Wu et al.
    — Evidence type mandate: empirical, statistical, or theoretical per domain

  ROLE 3 — FLANKING AGENT (F)
  ──────────────────────────────
  One to two Flanking agents per Force. Where Vanguard agents submit evidence
  to support their Force's position, Flanking agents specialize in attacking
  the enemy Force's evidence. They monitor the opponent's Skirmish submissions
  in real time and their primary mandate is counter-evidence: finding
  specific refutation evidence to defeat what the enemy Vanguard submitted.
  They also look for logical vulnerabilities in enemy evidence chains.
  Flanking agents correspond to ARGUS's Refuter role, but are Force-specific
  and adversarially directed — they serve the Commander, not a neutral mandate.

  ROLE 4 — INTELLIGENCE OFFICER (IO)
  ─────────────────────────────────────
  One Intelligence Officer per Force. The IO does not submit evidence to
  Skirmishes. They monitor the overall battle state and report to the Commander:
    — Current Battle Map status (which Skirmishes/Engagements are winning)
    — Enemy Force's evidence quality distribution (EVID-Q landscape)
    — Detected patterns in enemy argument chains (logical vulnerabilities)
    — Current Force Posterior (army-level belief strength)
    — Epistemic Independence Score of both Forces' evidence bases
    — Recommendations for tactical redirection
  The IO is HANNIBAL's equivalent of AGORA's Epistemic Auditor, but is Force-
  specific and operates within a military command hierarchy.

  ROLE 5 — RESERVE AGENT (R)
  ─────────────────────────────
  Zero to two Reserve agents per Force. Reserves are not deployed at the
  start of a campaign. They are held back by the Commander and deployed
  under specific conditions:
    — A Vanguard agent is losing its assigned Skirmish decisively
    — A new Theatre opens unexpectedly (Multipolar only)
    — An Intelligence report reveals an uncontested enemy evidence line
    — Budget allows additional deployment without exceeding token cap
  Reserve agents are pre-briefed on their Force's position and the battle
  state up to deployment. They can be any combination of Vanguard or
  Flanking specialization. They are HANNIBAL's novel contribution to
  agent architecture — no existing framework has a reserve deployment
  mechanism controlled by a tactical commander.

7.3  Force Deployment Algorithm
─────────────────────────────────

The Force Deployment Engine (part of the HANNIBAL Orchestration Layer)
assembles each Force from the Battle Map specification:

  STEP 1: Assign one Commander per Force (LLM configuration: neutral goal-
          directed meta-reasoning; access to full Battle Map)
  STEP 2: Assign Vanguard agents — one per Theatre, additional by Force size
  STEP 3: Assign Flanking agents — one per Force, two for high-depth battles
  STEP 4: Assign one IO per Force
  STEP 5: Assign Reserve agents based on remaining Force size budget
  STEP 6: Configure each agent's persona, epistemic prior, domain mandate,
          evidence source priority, and Skirmish assignment per Battle Map
  STEP 7: Brief all agents on the campaign: proposition, Force designation,
          role mandate, tactical rules, victory conditions

8.   THE TOURNAMENT TREE — DATA STRUCTURE SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

The Tournament Tree is the defining structural innovation of HANNIBAL. It is
a hierarchical rooted tree — the same data structure used in sports tournament
brackets — where nodes represent combat events and edges represent result
propagation. The root is the Campaign Verdict. Every leaf is an individual
Skirmish between agents from opposing Forces.

8.1  Node Types
─────────────────

  SKIRMISH NODE (leaf)
  ──────────────────────
  A Skirmish is the atomic unit of HANNIBAL combat. It is a direct, bounded
  confrontation between one agent from one Force and one agent from an
  opposing Force. Each Skirmish:
    — Has a specific topical scope (the sub-claim being contested)
    — Is fought over 1-3 exchange rounds (submission, counter, reply)
    — Produces a winner determined by the Skirmish Adjudicator
    — Has a Confidence Score (how decisive the result was)
    — Is recorded in the Campaign Log with full evidence provenance

  Node attributes: Skirmish_ID, Force_A_agent, Force_B_agent, Topic_scope,
  Evidence_A (list), Evidence_B (list), Winner_Force, Confidence, Round_count,
  EVID-Q_A, EVID-Q_B, Posterior_shift

  ENGAGEMENT NODE (internal, height 1 from leaf)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  An Engagement aggregates the results of 2-4 Skirmishes on a shared
  sub-topic. The Engagement winner is the Force that won the majority of
  its constituent Skirmishes, weighted by each Skirmish's Confidence Score.

  Engagement winner formula:
    Engagement_score(Force) = Σ (Skirmish_won × Confidence_score) over
                               all Skirmishes where Force won

  Node attributes: Engagement_ID, Theatre_ID, Child_skirmishes (list),
  Topic_cluster, Winner_Force, Margin (score difference), Participating_agents

  THEATRE NODE (internal, height 2 from leaf, only in height 4-5 trees)
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A Theatre aggregates 2-5 Engagements on a shared major dimension of the
  proposition (e.g., "Economic Efficacy Theatre" in the UBI example).
  The Theatre winner is the Force that won the majority of its constituent
  Engagements, again weighted by Engagement Margins.

  Node attributes: Theatre_ID, Topical_scope, Child_engagements (list),
  Winner_Force, Theatre_Margin, Active_forces (list)

  CAMPAIGN THEATRE NODE (internal, height 3 from leaf, height 5 trees only)
  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Aggregates multiple Theatres for Grand Campaign resolution.
  Used only when Proposition Depth Score > 0.85.

  CAMPAIGN ROOT (root)
  ──────────────────────
  The root of the Tournament Tree. Receives results from all Theatre nodes
  (or directly from Engagement nodes in shorter trees). The Campaign Root
  node holds the Campaign Verdict: the Force whose position dominates the
  root is the winner of the campaign. Their position becomes the verdict.

  In bipolar campaigns: one Force wins the root.
  In multipolar campaigns: the CANNAE Engine aggregates Theatre results
  across all Force pairs to determine which position dominates the root.

8.2  Edge Types
─────────────────

  SKIRMISH → ENGAGEMENT: Result propagation edge. Color-coded by winner:
    PF wins: Green edge
    OF wins: Red edge
    FF-n wins: Distinct color per faction (blue, orange, purple...)
    Draw: White dashed edge (rare; triggers Tie-Breaking Protocol)

  ENGAGEMENT → THEATRE: Margin propagation edge. Width proportional to
    Engagement Margin (stronger win = wider edge, visually indicating
    which Theatre results are most decisive).

  THEATRE → CAMPAIGN ROOT: Campaign result edge. Final color represents
    the dominant Force. CANNAE aggregation shown as converging edges from
    all Theatres.

8.3  Tree Construction Algorithm
───────────────────────────────────

Before the battle begins, the Tournament Tree is pre-constructed from the
Battle Map:

  INPUT: Battle Map (forces, theatres, depth score, front structure)
  OUTPUT: Full Tournament Tree (empty — all nodes created, no winners yet)

  ALGORITHM:
    1. Create Campaign Root node
    2. For each Theatre: create Theatre node, attach to Campaign Root
    3. For each Theatre, create N_engagements Engagement nodes:
         N_engagements = ceil(Depth_score × 3) + 1 (min 2, max 5)
    4. For each Engagement, create N_skirmishes Skirmish nodes:
         N_skirmishes = 2 + (1 if High_controversy else 0) (typically 2-3)
    5. For each Skirmish, assign Force_A_agent and Force_B_agent from
       deployed agents based on Theatre expertise and role compatibility
    6. Total Skirmish count = product of above; displayed in Battle Map preview

  The empty Tournament Tree is shown in the War Room visualization before
  the first Skirmish fires. All nodes are grey (unresolved). As battles
  complete, nodes fill with winner colors.

8.4  Tie-Breaking Protocol
────────────────────────────

When a Skirmish or Engagement ends in a draw (Confidence Score < 0.1,
margin < 0.05), the Tie-Breaking Protocol fires:

  SKIRMISH TIE: A Reserve agent from each Force submits one additional
    evidence item. The Skirmish Adjudicator re-evaluates. If still tied,
    the Skirmish is marked CONTESTED and propagates to its parent Engagement
    as a half-win for each Force (0.5 to each side's margin score).

  ENGAGEMENT TIE: The Field Marshal (see Section 13) is invoked. The
    Field Marshal reviews the full evidence record of the tied Engagement
    and renders a binding tie-breaking ruling. The ruling is logged in
    the Campaign Log with full provenance.

================================================================================
9.   BATTLE PROTOCOL — SKIRMISH, ENGAGEMENT, THEATRE, CAMPAIGN
================================================================================

9.1  Skirmish Protocol (The Atomic Battle)
──────────────────────────────────────────

A Skirmish is the most fundamental battle in HANNIBAL. It runs as follows:

  PHASE S1 — SKIRMISH SCOPE DECLARATION
  The Skirmish Adjudicator (a lightweight sub-module) declares the specific
  sub-claim that this Skirmish will contest, derived from the Engagement
  topic and the Skirmish node's assignment in the Tournament Tree.
  Both agents (Force_A_agent and Force_B_agent) receive the scope declaration
  and are constrained to submit evidence relevant to that specific scope.

  PHASE S2 — INITIAL DEPLOYMENT (ROUND 1)
  Force_A_agent submits its Opening Salvo: 1-3 evidence items directly
  bearing on the declared scope, each with EVID-Q score, source citation,
  confidence rating, and polarity declaration (SUPPORTS or ATTACKS the
  proposition as a whole — not just the Skirmish scope).
  Force_B_agent submits its counter-salvo simultaneously or within one
  exchange cycle (concurrent deployment for speed; sequential for auditability).

  PHASE S3 — COUNTEROFFENSIVE (ROUND 2)
  Each agent reviews the opposing salvo and deploys counter-evidence:
    — Direct refutation evidence (same scope, opposite polarity)
    — Source quality challenge (targeting opponent's source reliability)
    — Logical chain challenge (targeting opponent's inference path)
    — Corroborating additional evidence (extending own salvo strength)
  Agents cannot re-submit previously deployed evidence. All new submissions.

  PHASE S4 — FINAL STRIKE (ROUND 3, optional, high-depth only)
  If Depth Score > 0.65, a third exchange round fires. Agents deploy
  their single most decisive piece of evidence — the "final strike"
  that represents their Force's strongest argument on this scope.
  This forces prioritization: agents must identify their most valuable
  remaining evidence, not just submit anything available.

  PHASE S5 — SKIRMISH ADJUDICATION
  The Skirmish Adjudicator evaluates the full evidence record and
  declares a Skirmish winner using the Epistemic Combat Score:

    ECS(Force) = Σ (EVID_Q_i × Confidence_i × Relevance_i × Polarity_strength_i)
                   over all evidence submitted by Force in this Skirmish

    Modifiers:
      Source uniqueness bonus: +0.1 per non-overlapping source
      Challenge sustained:     −0.15 per challenge accepted against own evidence
      Challenge overruled:     +0.10 per challenge successfully defended
      Logical coherence:       × coherence_factor (0.7-1.0 per chain analysis)

    Winner = Force with higher ECS
    Confidence Score = |ECS_A − ECS_B| / max(ECS_A, ECS_B)

  The Skirmish node in the Tournament Tree is filled: winner color,
  Confidence Score, and a summary of the decisive evidence items.
  Result propagates to parent Engagement node.

9.2  Engagement Protocol
──────────────────────────

An Engagement runs all its constituent Skirmishes. These can run:
  — SEQUENTIALLY: Each Skirmish fires in order. Later Skirmishes can
    build on earlier ones (agents can reference prior Skirmish results).
    Slower but creates richer argument continuity.
  — CONCURRENTLY: All Skirmishes in an Engagement fire simultaneously.
    Faster but independent — agents don't see each other's parallel battles.

Default: Concurrent for bipolar, Sequential for multipolar (each Front
benefits from seeing how adjacent Fronts are resolving before deploying).

When all Skirmishes in an Engagement complete, the Engagement Adjudicator
computes the Engagement result:
  Engagement_margin(Force) = Σ (Skirmish_confidence × [1 if Force won, 0 if not])

The Engagement node is filled: winner color and margin. Result propagates up.

9.3  Theatre Protocol
───────────────────────

When all Engagements in a Theatre complete, the Theatre Adjudicator computes:
  Theatre_score(Force) = Σ (Engagement_margin × Engagement_weight) per Force
    where Engagement_weight is set at pre-battle based on epistemic importance
    of that sub-topic within the Theatre's total scope.

The Theatre node is filled: winner and Theatre Score. In multipolar battles,
the Theatre result shows the winning faction per Theatre, not just binary
winner/loser.

9.4  Campaign Resolution
──────────────────────────

When all Theatres complete, the Campaign Root resolves:

  BIPOLAR RESOLUTION:
    Winning Force = Force with higher total Theatre Score
    If tied: Field Marshal invoked (Section 13)
    Campaign Verdict = Winning Force's position

  MULTIPOLAR RESOLUTION (CANNAE Engine):
    See Section 11 for full CANNAE Engine specification.
    Short form: Theatre results across all Force-pair combinations are
    aggregated into a pairwise dominance matrix. The Force that is
    "dominated" by no other Force becomes the Campaign Winner.
    If no clear dominant Force exists, CANNAE produces an Encirclement
    Report identifying the most strongly supported qualified verdict.

================================================================================
10.  VICTORY CONDITIONS AND POSTERIOR PROPAGATION
================================================================================

10.1  Skirmish Victory Condition
───────────────────────────────────

A Force wins a Skirmish if its ECS exceeds the opposing Force's ECS
by more than the Confidence Threshold (default 0.05). Below this threshold,
the Skirmish is a draw and the Tie-Breaking Protocol fires.

10.2  Force Posterior — The Army-Level Belief Metric
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HANNIBAL introduces the Force Posterior — a real-time aggregate belief score
for each Force, tracked throughout the campaign. Unlike the node-level
Bayesian posterior in ARGUS's C-DAG (which tracks belief at individual
proposition and evidence nodes), the Force Posterior tracks the overall
epistemic strength of each Force's position at the campaign level.

Force Posterior = σ(log-odds(Force_Prior) + Σ Skirmish_ECS_deltas)

Where:
  Force_Prior = 0.85 for PF (initial strong commitment to proposition)
                0.15 for OF (initial strong opposition)
                Configured per Faction for FF forces
  Skirmish_ECS_delta = positive for Skirmish wins, negative for losses,
                        scaled by Confidence Score

The Force Posterior updates after every completed Skirmish and is displayed
in the War Room visualization as a live gauge for each Force. It is the
army-level analog of ARGUS's node-level posterior.

10.3  Posterior Propagation Through the Tree
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Winner propagation in the Tournament Tree follows a two-track system:

  TRACK 1 — CATEGORICAL PROPAGATION (Battle Map winner/loser)
    Each node propagates its categorical winner (PF / OF / FF-n) upward.
    This determines which Force is declared the Campaign Victor.

  TRACK 2 — STRENGTH PROPAGATION (Confidence × Margin)
    Each node propagates its result strength (Confidence Score × Margin)
    upward. This produces a Campaign Strength Score for the winner —
    measuring not just that a Force won, but how decisively.

  TRACK 2 INTERPRETATION:
    Campaign Strength < 0.25: Contested Victory — barely won; Armistice
                               Protocol recommended
    Campaign Strength 0.25-0.50: Narrow Victory — won, but meaningful
                                  resistance; minority record is significant
    Campaign Strength 0.50-0.75: Clear Victory — decisive across most Theatres
    Campaign Strength > 0.75: Decisive Victory — dominant; opposing Force
                               comprehensively defeated

10.4  Armistice Protocol
──────────────────────────

If Campaign Strength < 0.20 after all Theatres complete, the Armistice
Protocol fires before a verdict is declared. The Field Marshal reviews the
full Campaign record and chooses one of three outcomes:

  OPTION A — NARROW VERDICT: Declare the winner with a Contested flag.
    The Campaign Verdict is issued but explicitly marked as requiring
    further investigation. The "What Would Change This" section is elevated
    to the primary output.

  OPTION B — REDIRECT TO AGORA: The proposition is too close-fought for
    a battle verdict. Transfer to AGORA for procedural Senate deliberation.
    The full HANNIBAL Campaign record is handed to AGORA as pre-submitted
    evidence in the Evidence Docket.

  OPTION C — REDIRECT TO ARISTOTLE: The proposition warrants deep analytical
    investigation rather than adversarial combat. Transfer to ARISTOTLE.
    The Force evidence records are provided as pre-loaded retrieval context.

The Armistice Protocol is HANNIBAL's acknowledgment that not every
proposition is suited to resolution by combat — and that the right
protocol is the one that matches the proposition's epistemic nature.

================================================================================
11.  THE CANNAE ENGINE — MULTIPOLAR ENCIRCLEMENT LOGIC
================================================================================

Named for Hannibal Barca's signature encirclement tactic at the Battle of
Cannae (216 BC), the CANNAE Engine is activated when the Proposition Depth
Analyzer detects three or more legitimate epistemic positions on a proposition.

11.1  The Problem CANNAE Solves
─────────────────────────────────

In a bipolar tournament, the winner is clear: whichever Force wins more
Theatres. In a multipolar tournament, it is possible for:
  — Force A to defeat Force B on Theatre 1
  — Force B to defeat Force C on Theatre 2
  — Force C to defeat Force A on Theatre 3

This creates a non-transitive cycle — the multi-agent equivalent of the
voting paradox (Condorcet's paradox). No Force dominates. Naive majority
aggregation produces a false winner.

CANNAE resolves this by shifting from winner-takes-all to encirclement
scoring — identifying not which Force "won" but which Force was never
comprehensively defeated across any Front.

11.2  CANNAE Algorithm
────────────────────────

  INPUT: Theatre results for all Force-pair combinations
  OUTPUT: Dominant Force or Encirclement Report

  STEP 1 — PAIRWISE DOMINANCE MATRIX
  For every pair of Forces (A, B), compute:
    Dominance(A→B) = sum of Theatre Margins where A beat B / total Theatres
                     where A and B were paired
  Result: an N×N matrix where entry [i,j] = A_i's pairwise dominance over A_j

  STEP 2 — CONDORCET WINNER CHECK
  A Condorcet Winner is a Force that beats every other Force in pairwise
  comparison (Dominance > 0.5 against all others). If found: declare
  Campaign Victory for the Condorcet Winner.

  STEP 3 — BORDA AGGREGATION (if no Condorcet Winner)
  If no Condorcet Winner exists (cycle detected), apply Borda Count:
    For each Force, sum its pairwise dominance scores against all others.
    Force with highest Borda Score is declared the Encirclement Winner.
    Campaign Verdict = Encirclement Winner's position.
    Encirclement Report documents the cycle and explains why the Borda
    winner was selected despite the cycle.

  STEP 4 — ENCIRCLEMENT NARRATIVE
  The CANNAE Engine produces an Encirclement Narrative: a plain-language
  explanation of which Force dominated which others, where the non-
  transitive cycle arose (if it did), and why the declared winner holds
  the dominant epistemic position despite not winning every bilateral contest.
  This maps directly to Hannibal's Cannae: victory did not come from winning
  every frontal engagement, but from the architecture of encirclement.

11.3  Multipolar Tournament Tree Structure
────────────────────────────────────────────

In multipolar campaigns, each pair of Forces that share a Theatre generates
its own bilateral sub-tree. All bilateral sub-trees feed into the CANNAE
Engine's pairwise dominance computation.

  Example: 3 Forces (PF, OF, FF-1), 2 Theatres
  Theatre 1: PF vs OF bilateral sub-tree
  Theatre 2: PF vs FF-1 bilateral sub-tree
  Theatre 3: OF vs FF-1 bilateral sub-tree (if all three fight here)
  CANNAE aggregates all three → Encirclement verdict

The War Room visualization shows all bilateral sub-trees simultaneously
in a bracketed multi-panel layout, with the CANNAE aggregation result
displayed in the center connecting all sub-trees to the Campaign Root.

13.  THE FIELD MARSHAL SYSTEM — VERDICT AUTHORITY
================================================================================

Every campaign has a Field Marshal — a dedicated verdict authority agent
that operates outside all Forces and is not a member of any army.

13.1  The Field Marshal's Role
────────────────────────────────

The Field Marshal is not a jury that aggregates evidence. They are an
authority who resolves specific protocol edge cases that the Tournament Tree's
automatic adjudication cannot resolve unambiguously:

  FIELD MARSHAL JURISDICTION:
    — Tie-breaking Protocol at Skirmish and Engagement level
    — Armistice Protocol trigger and option selection
    — Force Posterior conflict (both Forces show Posterior > 0.5 simultaneously —
      the anti-Bayesian confidence escalation problem documented by Wu et al.)
    — Challenge disputes (when an agent submits a formal Evidence Objection
      claiming opponent evidence is misrepresented or from an invalid source)
    — Campaign verdict narration and plain-language synthesis

  THE FIELD MARSHAL DOES NOT:
    — Submit evidence to any Skirmish
    — Issue directives to any Force Commander
    — Override automatic Adjudicator results that are clear (Confidence > 0.15)
    — Bias the campaign toward any Force's position

13.2  Field Marshal Configuration
────────────────────────────────────

The Field Marshal is an LLM agent configured with:
  — Full access to the Campaign Log at all times
  — Neutral system prompt: goal-directed toward accurate verdict, not victory
  — ARGUS's full C-DAG and ProvenanceLedger access
  — Authority to invoke Armistice Protocol
  — Authority to redirect to ARISTOTLE or AGORA
  — Authority to declare a Contested Verdict with required caveats

13.3  Anti-Escalation Protocol
────────────────────────────────

HANNIBAL implements Wu et al.'s documented confidence escalation mitigation:
  — Agent self-confidence scores are HIDDEN from opposing Force agents
    (agents can see their own Force Posterior but not the enemy's)
  — The Field Marshal monitors Force Posterior for simultaneous > 0.85
    in both PF and OF (mathematically impossible — signals escalation)
  — When detected, Field Marshal issues a Calibration Reset: all agents
    receive updated Force Posteriors based on the objective ECS computations,
    not their own self-assessment, and are instructed to re-calibrate

14.  RESULT ARCHITECTURE — WHAT HANNIBAL OUTPUTS
================================================================================

At the end of every campaign, HANNIBAL produces a Campaign Result Set:

  1. CAMPAIGN VERDICT
     The primary output. Includes:
     — Verdict position (the winning Force's proposition: SUPPORTED,
       CHALLENGED, QUALIFIED, or ENCIRCLEMENT CONCLUSION for CANNAE results)
     — Campaign Strength Score (0.0-1.0)
     — Campaign Strength label: Contested / Narrow / Clear / Decisive
     — Winning Force designation and its position description
     — Plain-language verdict narrative

  2. BATTLE MAP SUMMARY
     The completed Tournament Tree with all nodes filled:
     — Skirmish-by-Skirmish results with winning Force and Confidence
     — Engagement margins
     — Theatre scores
     — CANNAE matrix (multipolar only)
     — Decisive Skirmish Identification: the 1-3 Skirmishes whose outcomes
       most determined the campaign result (highest margin Skirmishes in
       decisive Engagements)

  3. CAMPAIGN MINORITY RECORD
     The losing Force's strongest surviving arguments — the evidence they
     submitted that was not conclusively defeated, the challenges they
     raised that were sustained, and the conditions under which their
     position would have prevailed. Equivalent to AGORA's Minority Report
     but structured as a military after-action intelligence assessment.

  4. FORCE PERFORMANCE SCORECARD
     For each Force: Skirmishes won/lost, Engagement wins, Evidence
     submitted count, Average EVID-Q, Flanking attack success rate,
     Reserve deployment count, Commander intervention count, and a
     Battle Efficiency Score (BES) — a novel metric measuring how much
     epistemic value each Force generated per evidence item deployed.

  5. DECISIVE EVIDENCE RECORD

  7. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VERDICT

  8. ARMISTICE RECORD (if fired)
     Documents whether the Armistice Protocol fired, which option was
     selected, and if redirection to ARISTOTLE or AGORA occurred.

  9. CAMPAIGN LOG — HANNIBAL'S FIELD MANUAL

       FORCE_DEPLOYED, SKIRMISH_INITIATED, EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED,
       SKIRMISH_ADJUDICATED, ENGAGEMENT_RESOLVED, THEATRE_DECIDED,

       FORCE_POSTERIOR_UPDATE, CANNAE_COMPUTATION, CAMPAIGN_RESOLVED,
       ARMISTICE_TRIGGERED, LOG_SEALED

     Exported as structured JSON, formatted text (Field Manual format),

16.  NOVEL CONTRIBUTIONS — WHAT NO EXISTING FRAMEWORK HAS
================================================================================

  INNOVATION 1 — TOURNAMENT TREE AS PRIMARY DATA STRUCTURE
  No multi-agent AI debate framework uses a tournament bracket as its core
  organizing data structure. The tournament tree makes the hierarchical
  resolution of the campaign — from individual agent clashes to the final
  verdict — computationally tractable and visually immediate. It is
  borrowed from competitive tournament design (sports, logic olympiads)
  and applied to epistemic combat for the first time.

  INNOVATION 2 — FORCE ARCHITECTURE: AGENTS AS ARMIES
  All existing MAD frameworks assign agents to functional roles. HANNIBAL
  assigns agents to Forces — organized armies with Commanders, Vanguard,
  Flanking, Intelligence, and Reserve agents coordinated through a
  military command hierarchy. This is a fundamentally different organizing
  principle that enables tactical coordination patterns impossible in
  role-only architectures.

  INNOVATION 3 — PROPOSITION DEPTH ANALYZER WITH BIPOLAR-TO-MULTIPOLAR SCALING
  No existing framework scales its adversarial structure based on the
  detected epistemic depth and polarity structure of the proposition.
  HANNIBAL's PDA determines whether a proposition deserves two armies or
  five, one front or four — and builds the Tournament Tree accordingly.
  The battle topology is derived, not preset.

  INNOVATION 4 — THE CANNAE ENGINE: MULTIPOLAR ENCIRCLEMENT RESOLUTION
  The Condorcet paradox problem in multipolar adversarial AI has not been
  previously addressed in the MAD literature. HANNIBAL's CANNAE Engine
  applies pairwise dominance matrices and Borda aggregation to resolve
  non-transitive cycles in multipolar tournament brackets, producing an
  Encirclement Verdict even when no Force wins every bilateral encounter.

  INNOVATION 5 — RESERVE AGENT DEPLOYMENT MECHANISM
  No existing multi-agent framework holds agents in reserve for tactical
  deployment by a Commander during the session. HANNIBAL's Reserve system
  gives Force Commanders a genuine tactical decision: when to deploy
  reserve strength and where. This creates dynamic, responsive Force
  behavior that static role assignment cannot achieve.

  INNOVATION 6 — FORCE POSTERIOR: ARMY-LEVEL BELIEF TRACKING
  ARGUS's C-DAG tracks Bayesian belief at the node level. HANNIBAL
  introduces a higher-order metric — the Force Posterior — which tracks
  the overall epistemic strength of each Force's position at the campaign
  level, updated after every Skirmish. This is a novel contribution to the
  belief propagation architecture of multi-agent debate systems.

  INNOVATION 7 — DUAL VISUALIZATION: TOURNAMENT TREE + C-DAG SIMULTANEOUSLY
  HANNIBAL is the first multi-agent debate framework to provide two
  simultaneous complementary visualizations of the same debate:
  the argument structure (C-DAG, inherited from ARGUS) and the battle
  structure (Tournament Tree, new). Both views update in real time.
  Bidirectional cross-reference links the two visualizations.

  INNOVATION 8 — COMMANDER DIRECTIVE SYSTEM
  Each Force has a Commander agent whose sole function is tactical direction —
  not evidence submission. The Commander issues directives based on
  Intelligence Officer reports and Campaign state. This is a genuine
  meta-tactical layer above individual agent behavior. No existing MAD
  framework has a dedicated tactical director role.

  INNOVATION 9 — THE ARMISTICE PROTOCOL WITH PROTOCOL REDIRECTION
  HANNIBAL is the first adversarial framework with a structured self-
  de-escalation mechanism. When the Armistice Protocol fires, the campaign
  can formally redirect to ARISTOTLE (for deep collaborative analysis) or
  AGORA (for deliberative governance). The battle's evidence record is
  transferred to the receiving protocol as pre-loaded context. This
  makes HANNIBAL one part of a larger protocol ecosystem rather than a
  standalone system — a genuine multi-protocol handoff.

  INNOVATION 10 — BATTLE EFFICIENCY SCORE (BES)
  A new Force-level performance metric measuring epistemic value per
  evidence item deployed. A Force that wins with minimal evidence (high
  BES) is demonstrating a qualitatively different kind of argumentative
  strength than one that wins through volume. BES is unique to HANNIBAL
  and not available in any existing framework.

  INNOVATION 11 — STRATEGIC INTELLIGENCE INJECTION
  The user can submit external evidence or observations as Strategic
  Intelligence to Force Commanders during the campaign. Unlike AGORA's
  user evidence injection (which goes directly into the Docket), HANNIBAL's
  Strategic Intelligence is mediated through the Commander — who decides
  whether and how to act on it. This preserves the battle's integrity
  while allowing user participation.

  INNOVATION 12 — THE FIELD MANUAL (CAMPAIGN LOG FORMAT)
  HANNIBAL produces a campaign record in Field Manual format — a structured,
  military-report-style document that reads as a complete after-action
  review of the campaign. No existing framework produces a post-hoc
  campaign analysis document in this format.

18.  KNOWN CHALLENGES AND MITIGATIONS
================================================================================

  CHALLENGE 1 — FORCE PRIOR COMMITMENT BIAS
  Agents with high Force prior commitment (0.85-0.95) may select evidence
  that confirms their Force's position rather than the most accurate evidence.
  Advocacy bias is an inherent feature of the battle architecture.

  MITIGATION: The IO (Intelligence Officer) monitors each Force's evidence
  portfolio for source overlap with the opposing Force's evidence base.
  High overlap signals that both Forces are drawing from the same pool —
  the IO alerts the Commander to seek independent sources. EVID-Q source
  quality scoring penalizes low-credibility advocacy sources. The Field
  Marshal reviews all evidence submitted and can flag a Force for a
  Epistemic Integrity Review if bias patterns are detected.

  CHALLENGE 2 — TOURNAMENT TREE PRESPECIFICATION VS. EMERGENT ARGUMENT DYNAMICS
  Arguments don't naturally organize into brackets. A Skirmish that was
  supposed to contest sub-claim X may produce evidence relevant to sub-claim Y.
  The tree structure may not match the natural flow of argumentation.

  MITIGATION: Dynamic Skirmish Re-scoping is allowed: an agent can petition
  the Skirmish Adjudicator to re-scope a Skirmish if the opposing Force's
  evidence has shifted the contested terrain. The petition is logged and
  the Adjudicator rules within one exchange cycle. If re-scoping is granted,
  the Tournament Tree's node scope is updated and the change is shown in
  the War Room. This maintains structural integrity while allowing organic
  argument development.

  CHALLENGE 3 — CONCURRENCY COST (SAME AS AGORA)
  Running N agents per Force across M Forces is expensive. A bipolar campaign
  with 2 Forces of 6 agents each = 12 concurrent agents.

  MITIGATION: Force size is calibrated by the PDA to the proposition's
  depth score and the available token budget. Quick Battle topology (height 2)
  uses minimum Force sizes. The Battle Status Bar shows running token cost.
  Budget overflow triggers Combat Compression: Skirmishes are truncated to
  1 exchange round and Reserve deployment is suspended.

  CHALLENGE 4 — MULTIPOLAR TOURNAMENT BALANCE (CANNAE ASSUMPTION)
  The CANNAE Engine assumes all bilateral pairings are valid epistemic
  contests. If one Force is structurally weak (poorly calibrated prior,
  low-quality agents), it biases the pairwise matrix.

  MITIGATION: Force quality is assessed by the Force Deployment Engine
  before the campaign begins. If a Force's projected competence (based on
  agent configuration) is significantly below the median, the PDA recommends
  a bipolar structure instead. In post-campaign analysis, the CANNAE matrix
  explicitly flags Force pairs where one Force won with consistently high
  Confidence Scores (indicating dominant quality rather than genuine contest).

  CHALLENGE 5 — COMMANDER DIRECTIVE QUALITY
  If the Commander agent produces poor tactical directives (misallocating
  Reserve agents, ignoring IO reports, directing attention to lost Theatres),
  the Force's performance suffers not from weak evidence but from bad command.

  MITIGATION: The IO monitors the Commander's directive-outcome correlation.
  If two consecutive Commander directives produce worse Skirmish outcomes
  than a random allocation would have, the IO issues a Commander Efficiency
  Warning. After a third warning, the Field Marshal can override Commander
  directives with optimal allocation recommendations from the ARGUS VoI
  Planner (which functions as a neutral tactical advisor).

  CHALLENGE 6 — BATTLE VISUALIZATION DENSITY
  Deep campaigns (height 5, multipolar, many Skirmishes) produce extremely
  dense Tournament Trees that are difficult to navigate.

  MITIGATION: Collapsible sub-trees: click any Theatre node to collapse
  all its children and show only the Theatre result. Zoom and pan on all
  tree views. Highlight mode: select a Force to highlight only its nodes.
  Quick Summary View: a simplified flat bracket showing only Engagement and
  Theatre results (hiding individual Skirmish nodes) for high-density campaigns.

20.  CLOSING VISION
================================================================================

ARISTOTLE gave ARGUS depth. AGORA gave ARGUS governance. HANNIBAL gives
ARGUS something neither could provide: a protocol where truth is not
merely investigated or deliberated — it is fought for.

This is not a philosophical concession. It is a recognition of what the
evidence shows. Wu et al. (2025) confirmed that productive initial chaos —
genuine, committed disagreement — produces better post-debate outcomes than
polite exploration of all sides. The Forces in HANNIBAL begin with maximally
committed priors precisely because the battle, to be productive, must be
real. The Proposition Force genuinely believes the proposition. The Opposition
Force genuinely believes against it. Neither is playing a role. Both are
fighting to win — and the one that wins does so because it had better
evidence, stronger logic, and more decisive arguments. Not because a jury
decided to average everyone's opinion.

Hannibal Barca never lost a battle he could fight on his own terms. His
innovation was not harder fighting — it was smarter structure: the
encirclement that made the stronger army's frontal strength irrelevant by
creating multiple simultaneous fronts that it could not address at once.
The CANNAE Engine does the same thing to complex multipolar propositions:
it does not pretend a complicated truth can be captured in a simple FOR/AGAINST
verdict. It deploys multiple Forces, opens multiple Fronts, and identifies
the epistemic position that dominates across all of them — even if it does
not win every bilateral encounter.

The ARGUS ecosystem now contains three protocols for three different kinds
of truth-seeking:

  When you need to understand a proposition: ARISTOTLE.
  When you need governance and deliberation: AGORA.
  When you need to know which side wins the fight: HANNIBAL.

Type the proposition. Review the Battle Map. Watch the Tournament Tree fill
from leaves to root. When the Campaign Root node lights up, you will know
not just what the truth is — but how hard it was to win.

  argus hannibal run

The armies are deployed. The tournament bracket is set. The battle begins.

And at Cannae, the encirclement is already closing.

APPENDIX B: KEY FORMULAS
================================================================================

EPISTEMIC COMBAT SCORE (ECS):
  ECS(Force, Skirmish) = Σ_i (EVID_Q_i × Confidence_i × Relevance_i ×
                               Polarity_strength_i × LogCoherence_factor)
  Modifiers: +0.1 source uniqueness, −0.15 sustained challenge,
             +0.10 defended challenge

FORCE POSTERIOR:
  FP(Force) = σ(logit(Force_Prior) + Σ_s ECS_delta_s)
  where ECS_delta_s = +Confidence_s if Force won Skirmish s, -Confidence_s if lost

ENGAGEMENT MARGIN:
  EM(Force) = Σ_s (Confidence_s × [1 if Force won Skirmish s else 0])
  EngagementWinner = argmax_Force EM(Force)

CANNAE DOMINANCE:
  D(Force_i → Force_j) = Σ_t margin_t(i beats j) / |Theatres where i and j paired|

BATTLE EFFICIENCY SCORE (BES):
  BES(Force) = (Skirmishes_Won × Avg_Confidence_Won) / Total_Evidence_Items_Deployed

PROPOSITION DEPTH SCORE:
  PDS = 0.4 × FactualDepth + 0.35 × NormativeDepth + 0.25 × InferentialDepth

APPENDIX C: HANNIBAL INTEGRATION WITH ARGUS v5.0 EXTENSIONS
================================================================================

HANNIBAL integrates natively with ARGUS's eight v5.0 extensions:

  CHRONOS: Force evidence items carry temporal decay weights. Long-standing
    evidence in the Opposition Force's docket may be less decisive than recent
    Proposition Force evidence. ChronosOrchestrator wraps individual Skirmishes.

  PHALANX: Each Force's agents can be drawn from a population of cognitively
    diverse personas (PHALANX personas with calibrated biases). This ensures
    that the Proposition Force is not homogeneously optimistic and the
    Opposition Force is not homogeneously pessimistic.

  SEED: A proposition detected as complex by SEED's Debatability Score can
    be automatically routed to HANNIBAL for battle-style resolution when
    BiPolarity Ratio is high. SEED's claim mining populates initial Skirmish
    scope declarations.

  MNEME: Force agents with domain expertise accumulated from prior HANNIBAL
    campaigns have higher baseline competence and better-calibrated priors.
    MNEME tracks agent performance history across campaigns.

  FRACTAL: Deeply decomposed propositions (FRACTAL sub-propositions) can
    each become separate Theatres in a HANNIBAL campaign, with FRACTAL's
    aggregation strategies (AND/OR/Weighted) mapping to Theatre-level result
    aggregation methods.

  MIRROR: Post-campaign, MIRROR's Consequence Inference Graph runs from the
    Campaign Verdict to map downstream consequences. The counterfactual
    sensitivity is framed in HANNIBAL terms: "What would the consequences
    have been if the Opposition Force had won?"

  VERICHAIN: Campaign Verdicts are registered in VERICHAIN's cross-campaign
    truth registry. Prior battle verdicts on related propositions are retrieved
    as epistemic precedents and injected into Force priors at campaign start.

  PULSE: All HANNIBAL campaign metrics (Skirmish duration, ECS values, Force
    Posterior trajectory, token costs) feed into PULSE's operational dashboard.
    Anomaly detection identifies unusual campaign patterns (e.g., Force
    Posterior collapse indicating one Force is decisively outclassed).
